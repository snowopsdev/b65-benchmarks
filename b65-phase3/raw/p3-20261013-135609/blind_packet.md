## w02

### A

SEO gets you ranked. AEO gets you cited.

**SEO = Search Engine Optimization.** You optimize content for Google/Bing to rank higher in traditional search results. Focus: keywords, backlinks, technical health, and content that earns clicks.

**AEO = Answer Engine Optimization.** You optimize for AI assistants like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews to be the source they quote. Focus: clear, concise answers, structured data, FAQs, and authoritative, conversational content that can be summarized.

SEO drives traffic to your site. AEO drives brand mentions and trust when users never leave the AI.

### B

**SEO (Search Engine Optimization)** is about **visibility and traffic**. The goal is to rank high in search engine results pages (SERPs) so users click through to your website. It relies on keywords, backlinks, and technical site health to win the "click."

**AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)** is about **authority and immediacy**. With the rise of AI (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google SGE), users often receive a direct answer without ever visiting a website. AEO optimizes your content to be the *single source of truth* that AI engines synthesize and present as the definitive answer.

**The bottom line:** SEO drives users to your landing page; AEO ensures your brand is the answer the AI provides. SEO is for the browser; AEO is for the engine.

### C

SEO optimizes content to rank in traditional search results, driving clicks to your site. AEO optimizes for answer engines—AI assistants, chatbots, and featured snippets—so your brand is cited directly in the answer.

SEO targets keywords, backlinks, and page experience. AEO targets clarity, entity clarity, structured data, and source credibility.

In practice, SEO still matters because AI engines often draw from indexed, authoritative pages. But AEO shifts focus from “get clicked” to “get cited.” For marketers, that means creating concise, factual, well-structured content that answers questions directly and is easy for machines to verify.

### B

Depending on who you are talking to, here are a few ways to rewrite this, ranging from casual to professional:

**Option 1: Natural & Clear (Best for a quick message or email)**
> "Flash Attention's performance depends on the backend, which is why it worked well in one case but poorly in another."

**Option 2: Professional/Technical (Best for a report or documentation)**
> "The efficiency of Flash Attention varies by backend, which explains the discrepancy in performance between the two environments."

**Option 3: Concise (Best for a slide or a brief update)**
> "Flash Attention performance is backend-dependent, leading to inconsistent results across different setups."

**Key Improvements Made:**
*   **"The thing with..."** $\rightarrow$ Replaced with the actual subject (**"performance"** or **"efficiency"**).
*   **"Bad/Good"** $\rightarrow$ Replaced with more descriptive terms like **"poorly," "worked well,"** or **"discrepancy."**
*   **"On one... on other one"** $\rightarrow$ Clarified to **"in one case," "between the two,"** or **"across different setups."**

Flash Attention's performance is backend-dependent. That's why it performed poorly on one backend and well on another.
